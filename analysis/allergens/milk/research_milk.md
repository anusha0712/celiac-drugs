# Milk Proteins and Lactose as Hidden Drug Allergens — Research File

Every factual claim below is anchored to a primary-source URL inline. Claims
the original primer made that could not be verified against a primary source
have been removed. UNII, PMID, and CFR citations were independently verified
against FDA GSRS, PubMed, and Cornell / eCFR on 2026-04-21.

---

## Harm anchor

**Dry powder inhalers (DPIs) for asthma/COPD that carry lactose as an
excipient** are the drug-delivery category with (a) documented milk-protein
contamination of the excipient, (b) documented anaphylaxis in milk-allergic
patients, and (c) explicit "milk protein" contraindication language on
current FDA labels:

- Nowak-Wegrzyn A et al. "Contamination of dry powder inhalers for asthma
  with milk proteins containing lactose." J Allergy Clin Immunol
  2004;113(3):558-60. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15007361/
- Robles J, Motheral L. "Hypersensitivity reaction after inhalation of a
  lactose-containing dry powder inhaler." J Pediatr Pharmacol Ther
  2014;19(3):206-11. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25309152/
- Bar-On O et al. "Lactose-Containing Dry-Powder Inhalers for Patients with
  Cow's Milk Protein Allergy — The Conundrum." J Clin Med
  2022;11(24):7346. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36555964/

**Secondary harm anchor — intravenous methylprednisolone sodium succinate
(Solu-Medrol):**
- Hatcher VR et al. "Got milk? Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay analysis
  of casein proteins in methylprednisolone." Allergy Asthma Proc
  2025;46(5):431-437. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40958177/
  Detected trace Bos d 11 (casein) in all 5 vials tested; Bos d 9 (beta-
  lactoglobulin) undetectable.

**Recent case series framing the drug-vs-food anaphylaxis differential:**
- Gomez-Galán C et al. "Drug or food anaphylaxis?" Allergol Immunopathol
  (Madr) 2025;53(3):163-166. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40342124/
