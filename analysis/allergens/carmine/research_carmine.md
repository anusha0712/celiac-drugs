## Harm anchor

Greenhawt M, McMorris M, Baldwin J. "Carmine hypersensitivity masquerading
as azithromycin hypersensitivity." Allergy Asthma Proc 2009;30(1):95-101.
Woman experienced anaphylaxis ~90 min after generic azithromycin tablets;
workup identified carmine in the pink film coating as the culprit. She
tolerated the Pfizer Zithromax brand, which did not use carmine at the
time. This remains the only US-published case of medication-induced
carmine anaphylaxis traceable to a specific drug product.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19331724/

Khalil HA et al. Frontiers in Allergy 6:1661992 (Oct 2025). Carmine
elimination across food, cosmetics, AND medications resolved recurrent
anaphylaxis in a 39-year-old. Did not name a specific US drug product.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41113612/

Additional case literature:
- Takeo N et al. "Cochineal dye-induced immediate allergy: Review of
  Japanese cases and proposed new diagnostic chart." Allergol Int
  2018;67(4):496-505. Reviews 22 Japanese cases.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29705083/
- Sadowska B et al. "Carmine allergy in urticaria patients." Postepy
  Dermatol Alergol **2022**;39(1):94-100. (Primer misdated as 2020; correct
  year is 2022.) 8% of urticaria patients tested positive.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35369613/
- Weisbrod et al. "A Case Report of Allergic Hypersensitivity to Color
  Additives in Slurpee Beverages." Yale J Biol Med 2023;96(1):79-82.
  Food-additive case; included for context, not a drug case.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37009191/

## UNII verification

Only two UNIIs exist in FDA GSRS for the carmine family:

| UNII | Preferred name | Notes | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| TZ8Z31B35M | COCHINEAL | CAS 1343-78-8. Synonyms include "Cochineal Extract," Coccus Cacti Colorant, Dactylopius Coccus Colorant, E120, FEMA 2330. | https://precision.fda.gov/uniisearch/srs/unii/TZ8Z31B35M |
| CID8Z8N95N | CARMINIC ACID | CAS 1260-17-9; formula C22H20O13. Synonyms: C.I. Natural Red 4, C.I. 75470, E 120, cochineal carmine, cochineal red powder, sun red no. 1, carmine 5297. | https://precision.fda.gov/uniisearch/srs/unii/CID8Z8N95N |

**No standalone UNII for "CARMINE" exists in GSRS.** Carmine (the
aluminum/calcium lake of carminic acid) is captured in pharmaceutical
data via the CARMINIC ACID UNII.

**Valid alias strings (confirmed via GSRS synonym lists on CID8Z8N95N
and TZ8Z31B35M):** C.I. Natural Red 4; Natural Red 4; C.I. 75470 /
CI 75470; E 120 / E-120 / INS-120; cochineal carmine; cochineal red
powder; sun red no. 1; carmine 5297.

"Natural Red 2180" is NOT a GSRS-listed synonym and should not be used
as an authoritative search term.

## CFR and regulatory verification — the drug-labeling gap

| Citation | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 21 CFR 73.100 | Cochineal extract; carmine — FOOD labeling. Requires declaration by common or usual name ("cochineal extract" or "carmine") in the ingredient statement per §101.4. | 21 CFR 70.25 | Color-additive mixture labeling (generic). Applies to the color-additive container itself, not necessarily to the finished drug's consumer-facing label. | https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-70/subpart-B/section-70.25 |

FDAMA §412 inactive-ingredient labeling was implemented only for OTC drugs
via the March 17, 1999 Drug Facts final rule (64 FR 13254 et seq.,
codified at 21 CFR 201.66). Rx drug inactive ingredient disclosure remains
outside that mandate except for specific color-additive classes.

## FD&C Red No. 3 revocation — regulatory contrast (not carmine)

FDA published the order revoking FD&C Red No. 3 (erythrosine) color-additive
authorization on January 15, 2025. Compliance dates: food and beverages
Jan 15 2027; ingested drugs Jan 18 2028. Basis: Delaney Clause (rat
carcinogenicity). Red No. 3 is chemically unrelated to carmine (iodinated
xanthene vs. anthraquinone). Useful contrast: FDA has now acted on a
synthetic red dye for cancer, while the allergen-labeling gap for natural
carmine remains. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/16/2025-00830/
